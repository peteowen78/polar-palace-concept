Can people find Polar Palace online?

We looked at your website the way Google and a first-time visitor do. Here is what we found, in plain English, with no jargon.

5 of 10

Your site passes 5 of the 10 basic checks. Google can find you and the basics underneath are sound. Five gaps decide how you look once you are found, and the biggest is that your most useful facts are pictures rather than words.

The one that matters most

Your opening times, phone number and address cannot be read by Google.

Your opening times are a picture. A picture is easy for a parent to look at, but Google, AI assistants and screen readers cannot read the words inside it, so when someone asks "Is Polar Palace open now?" your website cannot answer.

Your home page also says "Open from 10am 7 days per week" and points to Facebook, while on Monday to Wednesday in term time you close at 2pm. A parent who trusts that line could arrive to a closed door.

Your phone number and address are not on the home page at all, and the site gives Google no fact sheet with your hours, prices, address or number. A new site would write all of these out as text and hand them to Google, so your hours show up directly in search.

Four smaller things to fix

All four are quick to put right.

To fix

Google is left to write your search listing

Under the blue link in a search result there are two lines describing the business. Your site does not supply them, so Google picks words from the page itself.

A new site would write them: indoor play, parties and Nerf Wars nights in Winsford, for ages 0 to 12.

To fix

Shared links show nothing

When a parent shares your site in a group chat, no picture, title or description goes with the link. For a party venue, that share is often how the invitation starts.

A new site would attach a bright photograph of the big slide and one clear line to every share.

To fix

Your home page has no main heading

Every page should have one main heading that says what it is. It is the first thing a search engine reads. Your home page has none; it starts straight into "Play Sessions", "Parties" and "Play Area".

A new site would open with a heading that says it plainly: indoor play and party centre, Winsford, Cheshire.

To fix

Your pictures are not described

Pictures can carry a short written description, used by image searches and read aloud to visitors who cannot see the screen. Only one of the twenty pictures on your home page has one.

A new site would describe every picture in plain words: the three lane slide, the football court, the party room.

Can AI assistants find you?

More people now ask ChatGPT, Claude or Google's AI answers instead of searching. These tools read your website too. We checked four things they rely on. Your site passes 1 of 4.

Working

AI assistants are allowed in

Nothing on your site turns them away.

To fix

The words can be read without running the page

There are words in the page, but your opening times, prices, party packages and menus are all pictures, so an AI tool reading your site would find almost none of the facts people ask about.

To fix

Your phone number and address are written out

Neither your phone number nor your address appears on your home page.

To fix

A summary file for AI tools

There is none. It is a newer idea, a short plain-text summary of what you offer, your hours, prices and how to book, and it takes about twenty minutes to write.

Writing your hours, prices and phone number out as words would fix three of these four at once.

Nobody can make an AI recommend a business, and anyone who offers to is selling nothing. What a website can do is let these tools reach it, read it and get your facts right.

What we would do
